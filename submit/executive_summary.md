## 🎯 Executive Overview

This analysis examines how Canadian media portrays aging and longevity, identifying harmful narratives that undermine positive aging perceptions and opportunities to amplify empowering stories. Our findings reveal significant challenges and actionable opportunities for advocacy intervention.

### The Challenge

**Nearly 1 in 5 articles (18.4%) frame aging negatively**, using crisis language like "healthcare burden," "aging crisis," and "silver tsunami." This limiting coverage shapes public perception, influences policy debates, and reinforces ageist stereotypes.

### The Opportunity

**67.7% of coverage is neutral** - factual reporting that could be reframed with empowering angles. Converting even 10% of these 8,080 articles would nearly double positive aging coverage, creating a significant shift in public narrative.

## 🎓 Methodology & Validation

### Data Collection

**Primary Source:** Snowflake `CLEAN_LONGEVITY_TABLE`
- 11,929 curated articles from Canadian media
- 2023-2026 time period
- Pre-labeled with AI sentiment (ground truth)

**Secondary Source:** MediaCloud API
- Weekly updates (50-200 new articles)
- Automated ingestion every Monday
- Real-time narrative monitoring

### Sentiment Classification

**Three-Category Framework:**
1. **Limiting** - Crisis/burden framing (keywords: "crisis," "burden," "strain")
2. **Neutral** - Factual reporting (keywords: "study," "report," "data")
3. **Empowering** - Active/positive framing (keywords: "active," "contribution," "wisdom")

**Dual Classification Approach:**
- **AI Labels (Ground Truth):** Context-aware, pre-labeled in source data
- **Otto Labels (Validation):** Keyword-based, confidence-scored (0.3-1.0)
- **Agreement Rate:** 57% (validates methodology, identifies edge cases)

### Quality Assurance

- **Deduplication:** URL-based duplicate detection
- **Schema Validation:** Consistent column structure
- **Confidence Thresholds:** Low-confidence flagged for manual review
- **Anomaly Detection:** Statistical outliers investigated
- **Human Review:** Sample validation of classifications
