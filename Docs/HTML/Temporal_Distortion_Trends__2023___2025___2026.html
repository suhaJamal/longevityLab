💡 Key Temporal Insights

  • 2025 Dip Explained: Accuracy dropped to 51.4% in 2025 (from 55.3% in 2023), suggesting increased media coverage volume (11,370 articles) may have diluted quality. However, 2026 shows recovery to 55.4%.
  • Limiting Phrases Improving Fastest: "Aging crisis" accuracy jumped from 29.8% (2023) to 70.8% (2026) — a remarkable +41% improvement. Media is learning to use crisis language more accurately.
  • "Aging Well" Remains Problematic: Despite being labeled "empowering," this phrase only improved from 18.1% to 22.9% accuracy. It's still the most misused phrase, suggesting performative positivity without substance.
  • "Healthy Aging" Success Story: Jumped from 50.8% to 87.5% accuracy in 2026 — the highest accuracy among empowering phrases. This shows media CAN use positive language accurately when they try.
  • British Columbia Decline: BC dropped from 66.5% (2023) to 54.0% (2026) — a -12.5% decline. This is concerning given BC was the most accurate province in 2023. Requires investigation into what changed.
  • Quebec Improving: Quebec improved from 52.0% to 60.0% (+8%), suggesting provincial advocacy efforts may be working. This is a model for other provinces.
  • Neutral Phrases Declining: Neutral phrase accuracy dropped from 68.5% to 56.6% (-11.9%). Media is increasingly using neutral language with hidden bias — the most insidious form of distortion.
  • Sentiment Improving: Average sentiment improved from -0.124 (2023) to +0.020 (2025), then back to -0.009 (2026). Overall trend is toward less negative framing, but volatility suggests inconsistent editorial standards.
  • Advocacy Impact Visible: The 2026 recovery (+4% accuracy) after 2025 decline suggests advocacy efforts are having an effect. Continued pressure is needed to maintain and accelerate progress.
